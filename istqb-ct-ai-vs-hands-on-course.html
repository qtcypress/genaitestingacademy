Comparison

ISTQB CT-AI
compared with a hands-on course

Written by people who sell one of the two, which you should factor in. The short version: if you need a recognised credential, buy the certification — this course is not a substitute for it, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling you something. If you need to be able to do the work, a syllabus will not get you there. They are different purchases for different problems.

Side by side

ISTQB CT-AI

A vendor-neutral certification with an exam and a published syllabus. Recognised — the name means something to recruiters, HR filters and tender documents, which is its central advantage and not a small one. Portable across employers and countries. Tests whether you know the concepts and the standard vocabulary. Does not involve testing a running application, and does not ask you to produce anything.

A hands-on course

Practice against applications you attack. Produces artefacts you can show — an evaluation suite, a golden dataset, a documented finding. Not recognised in the way a certification is: a provider's certificate carries only the weight of the work behind it, which means you have to show the work. Covers newer ground, because it is not bound to a revision cycle.

Doing both is the common answer

There is no tension between them and the budgets are different in size. A useful sequence, if you intend to do both, is the practical work first and the exam second: the syllabus is much easier to absorb once you have seen the things it names, and the concepts stop being abstractions to memorise. The reverse order works too and simply costs more study time.

The questions people actually ask

Is ISTQB CT-AI worth it?

It depends entirely on whether your problem is credibility or capability. CT-AI is ISTQB's specialist certification in AI testing, it is vendor-neutral, and it is the AI testing qualification that hiring managers and tender documents actually recognise — which is a real and specific kind of value. If you need a line on a CV that a recruiter screening on keywords will match, or you work somewhere that requires certified testers on a bid, it is the thing to buy and nothing else substitutes for it. What it is not is practice. It is a syllabus and a multiple-choice exam, so passing it demonstrates that you understand the vocabulary and the concepts, not that you have ever decided whether a real model's real output was a defect.

What is the difference between CT-AI and a hands-on AI testing course?

They answer different questions, which is why the comparison is less competitive than it looks. CT-AI answers "can this person discuss AI quality characteristics, ML performance metrics and the testing concerns of an AI system in standard terminology?" — and it answers that in a form an employer can verify. A hands-on course answers "has this person actually attacked a running application, found something, and written it up?" — and it answers that in a form an employer can read. The first is recognised and portable; the second is demonstrable but needs you to show your work. Neither covers the other's ground.

Which should I do first?

Start with whichever closes the gap you actually have. If applications are not getting past screening, the certification is the bottleneck and the practice will not fix it. If you are reaching interviews and then struggling with "tell me about a time you tested an AI feature", no certification will fix that, and only having done it will. One useful sequence if you intend to do both: the practical work first, then the exam. The syllabus is considerably easier to absorb once you have seen the things it names, and the concepts stop being abstractions you memorise.

Can a course replace the certification?

No, and this is the part worth being direct about. A certificate issued by a training provider is not an ISTQB certification, and nobody in the industry treats the two as equivalent. If an employer or a tender asks for CT-AI, the only thing that satisfies it is CT-AI, from an accredited provider, with the exam passed. Any course page claiming otherwise is selling you something.

Does CT-AI cover LLMs and generative AI?

Partly, and this is the most important thing to check for yourself before booking. The syllabus was built around machine-learning systems broadly — classifiers, model performance metrics, data quality, bias, the testing concerns of ML pipelines — and much of that generalises. Testing a RAG retrieval layer, evaluating generated text for groundedness, defending against prompt injection, and testing an agent that calls tools are newer concerns that move faster than any syllabus revision cycle can. ISTQB does revise its syllabi, so the honest answer is: read the current syllabus at istqb.org, which is published free, and see what is in the version on offer today rather than trusting any third-party summary of it, including this one.

How much does CT-AI cost and how long does it take?

This page does not quote a fee, a question count or a pass mark on purpose. Exam prices vary by country and by accredited provider, and all of these details change — a stale figure on a page about a certification is worse than no figure, because someone budgets or books on it. Go to istqb.org for the current syllabus and the list of accredited providers in your region, and get the fee from the provider you would actually sit with. What can be said is the shape: it is a foundation-level specialist certification, the syllabus is readable in evenings rather than months, and the main cost for most people is the exam rather than the study.
